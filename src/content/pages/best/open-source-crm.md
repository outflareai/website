## How we chose

We looked for CRMs whose code you can read, run and change, and compared them on:

- **Licence.** Checked in each project's own repository or licence page.
- **Self-hosting.** Can you run it on your own servers, free?
- **What it is for.** Sales pipeline, ERP suite, developer framework or marketing workspace.
- **Hosted option.** Whether the maker sells hosting, and from what price.

Every fact about another project was checked on its own website or repository in November 2026.

## The shortlist

### Twenty

A modern CRM that calls itself "the #1 Open Source CRM on GitHub". Its [licence](https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty) is mostly AGPL-3.0, with files marked Enterprise under a commercial licence. Its [pricing page](https://twenty.com/pricing) says self-hosting the open-source core is free; Cloud Pro is $9 per user a month, billed yearly (checked November 2026).

**Best for:** startups that want a modern sales CRM they can self-host.

### SuiteCRM

A long-standing open-source CRM, [published under AGPLv3](https://github.com/salesagility/SuiteCRM-Core), that you can host yourself or run as [SuiteCRM Hosted](https://suitecrm.com/).

**Best for:** organisations that want a full, configurable CRM with a large module set.

### EspoCRM

An open-source CRM [licensed under GNU AGPLv3](https://github.com/espocrm/espocrm), installable with Docker. [Cloud plans](https://www.espocrm.com/pricing/) start at $15 per user a month with a three-user minimum (checked November 2026).

**Best for:** small and mid-sized teams that want a lightweight, customisable CRM.

### Odoo CRM

The CRM app in the Odoo suite. [Odoo Community is LGPL-3.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/legal/licenses.html); Enterprise needs a subscription. Odoo Online offers [one app free](https://www.odoo.com/pricing) for unlimited users.

**Best for:** companies that also want accounting, inventory or other ERP apps.

### Krayin CRM

"A fully open-source CRM framework which will always be free under the [MIT License](https://github.com/krayin/laravel-crm)", built on Laravel (PHP).

**Best for:** PHP developers building a CRM for a client or a niche.

### Atomic CRM

A CRM [built with React, shadcn/ui and Supabase](https://github.com/marmelab/atomic-crm), MIT-licensed, by Marmelab.

**Best for:** developers who want a small, readable codebase to extend.

### Outflare

[Customer records](/product/customer-data/) inside an open-source marketing workspace (AGPL-3.0): people, companies and deals, with every email and id that points at them, duplicates and merges, a timeline, consent, and the full history of every field. [Smart lists](/docs/guides/smart-lists/) group records by rules. The same records feed cold email, the reply inbox, lifecycle email and outcomes, so a reply that books a meeting moves the deal. Import [from Attio](/docs/guides/move-from-attio/) once, or sync deals from [HubSpot](/docs/integrations/calendars-and-hubspot/).

**Best for:** founders and marketing teams who want the CRM next to outreach, analytics and AI agents. **Not the best pick** for a standalone sales CRM with heavy custom workflows and page layouts: Twenty, SuiteCRM or EspoCRM are built for that.

## What open source means here

AGPL-3.0 lets you run and change the code; if you offer a changed version to others over a network, you share your changes. MIT lets you do almost anything, including closed forks. Check the licence against how you plan to use the CRM.

With Outflare, your data sits in your own Postgres when you self-host. Neither the open-source engine nor Outflare Cloud lends you a data provider or an AI key: each workspace connects its own accounts. See [Open source and Cloud](/docs/project/open-source-and-cloud/).
